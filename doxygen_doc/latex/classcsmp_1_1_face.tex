\doxysubsection{Detailed Description}
\subsubsection*{template$<$uint32\+\_\+t dim$>$\newline
class csmp\+::\+Face$<$ dim $>$}
Lower dimensional surface (in 3D) or line (2D) elements serve as boundaries (\doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face}) or connect disconnected mesh domains (\doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_inter_face}{Inter\+Face}). The \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} is used for material interfaces that are welded together by sharing nodes. \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_boundary}{Boundary} objects of a CSMP model consist of \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} objects.

The \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} is rather similar in its functionality to the \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_element}{Element} with many member functions sharing their names. As an important difference, \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} objects know their higher-\/dimensional neighbor \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_element}{Element} objects. This means that \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} objects are not only connected to each other, but have an extra set of pointers that connect them to \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_element}{Element} objects.

\doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} objects also have a unit normal that helps with the application of tractions etc. Since the \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} is not inherited from \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_element}{Element}, it constitutes a separate type and can store unique variables, especially ones like tractions, influxes or transfer coefficients, which would unnecessarily consume storage if discretised evenly over a domain. \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_element}{Element} variables like permeability can still be accessed easily because the \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} has pointers to its inner and outer \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_element}{Element} neighbors.

\doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} objects have been designed to support operations on internal or external model boundaries. If a \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} is located at an external (\doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_model}{Model}) boundary its normal will point outward and only the inner higher-\/dimensional neighbor 0 will be initialised. Representing internal boundaries, \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} objects provide access to both neighbors, also helping to distinguish inside from outside domains. This distinction is possible and indicated by to the \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} normal that points to the outside. Thus, the \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_element}{Element} objects that the \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} normal points to are referred to as outside and the ones on the opposite side are the internal ones. This also means that the sense of node numbering of the \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} matches that of the face of the \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_element}{Element} on the inside.

\begin{DoxyNote}{Note}
without a topology it is impossible to "{}compute"{} what the inside or outside of a surface in a boundary representation is. This info comes from CAD or geomodel into CSMP++ which can only perform consistency checks.
\end{DoxyNote}
\begin{DoxyAttention}{Attention}
Since so much information is required to construct complete \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} objects, this is done in 2 steps\+: 1) the various constructors establish \doxylink{classcsmp_1_1_face}{Face} connectivity with higher-\/dimensional neighbors, associated indices and policies. 2) separate operations assign idx, nodes, neighbors, etc.
\end{DoxyAttention}

The main idea is to measure the height of the oriented bounding box (oriented by the direction of vec\+Direction) which surrounds the element. This is done by projecting each node onto the direction vector and calculating the difference between the largest and smallest magnitude.
